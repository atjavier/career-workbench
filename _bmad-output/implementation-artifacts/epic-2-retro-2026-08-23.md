# Epic 2 Retrospective: Compliant Job Discovery

Date: 2026-08-23  
Epic status: Complete  
Stories reviewed: 4/4 done

## Epic Review

Epic 2 delivered the local discovery foundation: editable eligibility preferences, policy-gated source configuration, bounded explicit refresh outcomes, and provenance-preserving normalized Job Listings. The epic remains local-first and does not perform unauthorized scraping, background retrieval, automatic retries, or browser automation.

### What went well

- Each story preserved the explicit-action boundary and kept discovery operations local, bounded, and auditable.
- Versioned immutable revisions were consistently used for preferences and source policy, with optimistic-concurrency protection.
- Refresh outcomes were persisted incrementally, allowing successful sources to remain visible when another source failed, blocked, or throttled.
- Job Listing normalization retained every Source Record, original URL, source revision, timestamps, and duplicate history instead of silently merging or deleting records.
- Review feedback materially strengthened the epic: persistence-boundary validation, source/run consistency, bounded adapter volumes, stale protection, accessible confirmation semantics, and truthful empty/partial states were all addressed.
- Accessibility was treated as a product requirement: native controls, labels, polite status announcements, focus restoration, keyboard operation, and non-color-only state communication recur across the delivered surfaces.

### Challenges and growth areas

- The source-policy and retrieval boundary required repeated hardening after implementation. Domain-only validation was insufficient; database constraints and read-time validation were necessary to keep persisted state truthful.
- Freshness, duplicate detection, source retention, and exact fit thresholds remain areas where product decisions must stay explicit as later epics consume the data.
- The codebase currently has an intentionally empty production adapter registry and a conservative manual-browser catalog. This is correct for the MVP but means future automated sources need a new policy review rather than assuming the existing seam is permission.
- Cross-cutting handoff discipline is useful: completed stories now point directly to the next workflow and maintain a clear file/change record.

### Quality and verification

The epic’s stories were validated with adversarial unit/integration/UI coverage and the repository’s full gates (`npm test`, typecheck, lint, and production build). Story 2.4’s final implementation reported 68 passing tests; subsequent work continued to pass the full suite. No production deployment or external source retrieval was introduced.

## Lessons Learned

1. Treat policy, provenance, and recovery as data invariants, not only UI behavior.
2. Preserve raw permitted attribution and immutable revisions so later decisions can be explained without reconstructing history.
3. Make uncertainty visible (`Unknown`, stale, partial, blocked) instead of filling gaps with optimistic assumptions.
4. Test prohibited behavior explicitly—network calls, retries, schedulers, credential reuse, and generic fetches—not just successful outcomes.
5. Accessibility and recovery semantics belong in the first implementation pass; retrofitting them is more expensive and less reliable.
